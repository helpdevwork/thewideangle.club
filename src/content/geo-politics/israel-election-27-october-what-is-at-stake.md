Israelis vote on 27 October 2026, and no poll I could open shows either side within reach of a majority. That means the headline, who finishes first, may matter less than who can assemble a government afterwards. The rules make that process longer and messier than most people expect.

## What is documented

The Knesset dissolved on 17 July 2026 (Wikipedia). All 120 seats are filled by closed-list proportional representation from a single nationwide list, the threshold is 3.25%, and 61 seats make a majority. About 7.3 million people are on the voter roll and 38 lists are on the ballot, of which about 11 to 15 are expected to pass the threshold (AIJAC). Official results are due by 4 November. The Joint List and Ra'am were initially barred by the Central Elections Committee and reinstated by the Supreme Court on 23 September. Voters choose a party list, not a prime minister. Britannica describes the vote as many observers' referendum on Benjamin Netanyahu's handling of 7 October and the war with Hamas.

## What the polls say

Polls disagree on details but agree on the shape. Three sources:

| Source | Largest party | Second | Bloc totals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walla poll, fieldwork 19 October (601 respondents, margin of error 4%), via Jerusalem Post | Yashar (Gadi Eisenkot) 23 | Likud (Benjamin Netanyahu) 19 | Opposition 54; coalition 48; unaligned about 18 |
| Seven-poll average, The New Arab, 20 October (updated 21 October) | Yashar 22 | Likud 21 | Eisenkot's opposition 51; pro-Netanyahu 51; Joint List and Ra'am 14 combined |
| Poll averages as of mid-October, AIJAC | Likud about 21 and Yashar about 21, roughly tied | | Netanyahu bloc about 49 to 55; opposition about 50 to 54 |

AIJAC adds that Yashar led Likud in 13 of 20 polls over the two weeks to mid-October, and that only Channel 14 has given Netanyahu's bloc 61. On the personal question of who should be prime minister, AIJAC lists Channel 13 (7 October) at Eisenkot 44%, Netanyahu 40%; Channel 12 (5 October) at 41% to 38%; and Channel 14 (early October) at Netanyahu 52%, Eisenkot 48%. A four-seat gap between the top two parties is within the Walla poll's own 4% margin of error.

Other developments reported by The New Arab: Benny Gantz's party withdrew after polling below the threshold, Donald Trump publicly endorsed Netanyahu, and the first poll after the endorsement did not clearly show a Likud gain.

## What the fight is about

AIJAC lists the main issues. The opposition wants a state inquiry into 7 October, which the government has resisted. Most opposition parties, along with Ofer Winter and Yoaz Hendel, want universal military conscription; the Haredi parties reject sanctions or quotas. The opposition wants to reverse the government's judicial changes, while the right wants more. Security, Gaza and Iran are the top issue for Jewish voters, the economy close behind, and crime is the top issue for Arab voters. AIJAC says no major party backs a near-term Palestinian state.

## The math nobody can escape

The previous Knesset's seats are a poor guide. Wikipedia lists Netanyahu's current bloc at 64 seats. The polls above put it at 48 to 55. The opposition does better but still needs partners, and the sources disagree on who those partners are. The New Arab calls the Joint List and Ra'am, projected to rise from 10 to 14 seats, potential kingmakers; AIJAC says both main camps have ruled out bringing them into a coalition.

Here is how the process works after the vote, per AIJAC. Results are published by 4 November. President Isaac Herzog nominates a candidate within seven days of the results, after consulting each party. That nominee has four weeks to build a coalition, extendable by two. The government needs more yes than no votes in a confidence vote, not 61, so a minority government is possible. If the first attempt fails, a second candidate gets 28 days. If everything fails, the Knesset dissolves and elections are held within 90 days, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker. The precedent is 2021, when Likud won 30 seats but could not form a government and Naftali Bennett became prime minister with seven.

## Why India should care

I am not citing numbers in this section, because I have not verified any trade or deal figures for this post; this is analysis. India has defence and technology ties with Israel and has backed the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC). A fragile coalition, or a long stalemate, tends to slow big strategic decisions, and the timeline above means that could run into December. India also has a large diaspora across West Asia whose safety depends on regional stability. The outcome is likely to affect the pace of regional diplomacy; that is my judgement, not a sourced finding.

## My read

Eisenkot is more likely than not to finish first on seats, but that is the least interesting part. The real fight is the coalition arithmetic. If the Arab parties win 14 seats and neither camp will work with them, the realistic outcomes are a minority government, a grand-coalition push, or another election within 90 days. I would bet on weeks of talks and no clean answer on 28 October. What would change my mind: a final-week shift that pushes the Netanyahu bloc past 55 in several independent polls, which would make his path to 61 plausible.

## What to watch next

- 27 October 2026: voting day and exit polls.
- 4 November 2026: official results due.
- Within seven days of results: President Herzog's nomination of a candidate.
