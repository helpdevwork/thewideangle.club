The company that physically makes most of the world's advanced AI chips just told investors that demand is "still far above our capability today to supply." Its stock, and much of the chip sector, fell anyway. That gap between what the factory floor says and what the market feels is the real story of this earnings season.

## What TSMC reported

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) reported third-quarter 2026 results on 15 October 2026. It had already flagged the revenue number a week earlier, on 8 October.

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2026 revenue | US$46.7 billion (NT$1.49 trillion), above management's own range of US$44.6–45.8 billion | Motley Fool, Invezz, Startup Fortune |
| Revenue growth | 41% year on year in US dollars; about 50% in Taiwan dollars | Motley Fool, Invezz |
| Net income | NT$763.06 billion, up 68.7% year on year; sixth straight record | Quartz |
| Q4 2026 revenue guidance | US$52.3–54.3 billion | Motley Fool, Quartz |
| 2026 revenue growth outlook | about 44% in US dollars, up from a little over 40% in July | Motley Fool, Quartz |
| AI and high-performance computing share of revenue | 68%, versus 57% in Q3 2025 | Motley Fool |

Two comparisons are worth doing yourself. The midpoint of Q4 guidance (US$53.3 billion) is about 14% above Q3 revenue. And net income of NT$763.06 billion beat the NT$740.8 billion that LSEG had projected, according to Startup Fortune, by about 3%. A company bracing for a slowdown does not guide like this.

## What the CEO said

According to the Motley Fool's account of the call, CEO C.C. Wei said "we did not see any sign of slowdown" in AI demand, and that growth "probably will be higher." TSMC also expects its AI-accelerator revenue to compound at a mid-to-high 50% annual rate over 2024–2029.

The capacity story supports him. Startup Fortune reports that TSMC's advanced packaging capacity (the CoWoS process that bonds AI chips to memory) is reportedly booked into 2027, and that Nvidia has reportedly locked up about 60% of TSMC's 2026 CoWoS capacity. Those are reported figures from analyst and press estimates, not TSMC disclosures, so treat them as indicative. Its customers named in the coverage include Nvidia, AMD and Apple.

## Where the nerves come from

The same Motley Fool piece notes that Nvidia, TSMC's biggest customer, closed at US$217.23 on 15 October, about 9% below its record close of US$239.24 on 6 October. A third-party blog summary on KuCoin (not TSMC's own filing, so I treat it with caution) reports that TSMC's US-listed shares fell about 1.8% on 15 October and 2.3% on 16 October, and puts the selloff down to concerns over AI infrastructure financing, a run of daily declines in the Philadelphia semiconductor index, and 2nm ramp costs.

Meanwhile Nvidia's most recent reported quarter (ended 26 July 2026) showed revenue of US$96.2 billion, up 106% year on year, with guidance of roughly US$108 billion for the next quarter (Motley Fool).

So the demand data is accelerating while prices drift lower. Both can be true at once: the supply chain is full, and investors are asking whether the customers paying for all this capacity will earn it back.

## The money TSMC is spending

Startup Fortune reports 2026 capital spending guidance of US$60–64 billion, raised from US$52–56 billion, with 70–80% going to advanced nodes. Goldman Sachs, per Invezz, lifted its 2027 and 2028 capex forecasts to US$85 billion and US$98 billion. When the company that sells the picks and shovels spends that much, it is telling you what it expects to sell.

## Why it matters, including for India

Almost every AI model you use, from chatbots to the recommendation engines behind Indian apps, runs on chips that one company in Taiwan manufactures. When it says it cannot build enough, three things follow. Cloud prices for AI stay high. Countries and firms without chip access wait longer. And any disruption in Taiwan becomes a global economic risk. For India's semiconductor ambitions, the lesson is that capacity, not demand, is the scarce resource, and it takes years to build.

## My read

The factory is the better witness. Orders, guidance and a raised full-year outlook are hard numbers TSMC will be held to; a falling share price is a mood. I would bet that this quarter marks a valuation reset, not a demand reversal. What would change my mind: TSMC trimming its Q4 guide, or a major customer publicly cutting its capital spending. Neither shows up in the sources I checked. If you own AI-linked stocks, the question to ask is not "is demand real?" but "is it priced in?"

## What to watch next

- Nvidia's next quarterly results, expected later in November 2026 (confirm the date on Nvidia's investor page).
- Whether TSMC's Q4 revenue lands inside its US$52.3–54.3 billion range when it reports in January 2027 (confirm the date).
- Hyperscaler capital-spending updates through this earnings season.
