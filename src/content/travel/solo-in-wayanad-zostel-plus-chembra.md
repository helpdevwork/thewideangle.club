Four days, one solo trip, and almost none of it paid for out of pocket. The plan was Bangalore to
Kalpetta in Wayanad — Thursday and Friday spent at Zostel Plus Wayanad up near the Chembra Estates,
and Saturday-Sunday given over to a full weekend circuit of caves, waterfalls, and a couple of quieter
spots that don't make every Wayanad itinerary. Both legs of travel, the first stay, and most of the
second one ran on Scapia coins, which turned what could've been an expensive four days into one of the
cheapest trips I've done all year.

## Where I stayed: Zostel Plus Wayanad

Most people default to a stay in Kalpetta or Meppadi town when they think of Wayanad, but Zostel Plus
Wayanad isn't in either — it's tucked up near the Chembra Estates, well outside the town noise, and
that location alone does most of the heavy lifting for why the property works as well as it does.
Waking up to tea estate rows instead of a town street changes the entire pace of a stay.

The property backs that location up with a genuinely strong set of inclusions. In-house dining meant
never having to leave for meals, and the estate cycling route — a guided ride through the Chembra
tea gardens for ₹600 — was worth every rupee for the views alone. There's also a private waterfall on
the property that guests get into for free, which is the kind of thing that would be a paid add-on
almost anywhere else. Wifi was fast enough to work off if needed, the community manager kept the whole
place running smoothly and made a genuine effort to get guests talking to each other, and the social
layout of the property meant a solo trip never actually felt solo — I ended up building a decent
rapport with a handful of other travelers from completely different backgrounds over the two nights,
which is exactly the kind of thing a good hostel is supposed to deliver and a lot of them don't. If a
Wayanad trip is on the cards and a hostel stay works for the itinerary, Zostel Plus Wayanad is worth
booking specifically for that Chembra-side location over anything closer to town.

## Chembra Peak: book the tickets early

Chembra Peak is the trek to do while based here, and it's an easy recommendation — the heart-shaped
lake partway up is the postcard shot, but the climb itself through shola forest and open grassland is
worth doing on its own terms. The one thing that actually matters logistically: forest department
tickets are capped per day and sold through an online portal, and they run out well before trek day if
booked late. Anyone planning a Wayanad trip around this peak should lock in the slot as soon as dates
are fixed rather than assuming walk-in tickets will be available.

## Nostalgic – 1980s Restaurant

One meal from this trip is worth calling out on its own: Nostalgic, a restaurant built entirely around
a 1980s theme, from the décor down to the music playing overhead. The food matched the setting — this
was easily one of the better meals of the whole trip, and the kind of place that's worth a detour even
if it isn't the closest option to wherever you're staying. Banana chips picked up locally for snacking
were the other small highlight — Wayanad's version has a noticeably good crunch and flavor compared to
the chips available back home, and a bag or two is an easy thing to carry back.

## The weekend circuit: caves, waterfalls, and two quieter stops

Saturday and Sunday were spent at a different stay (not worth detailing here), with the days given
over to covering as much of Wayanad's spread-out sights as possible.

**Edakkal Caves** were the first stop — Neolithic petroglyphs cut into a natural cleft in the rock,
reached by a steep but short climb, with views over the Wayanad valley from the top that are worth the
climb on their own. **Soochipara Falls**, a three-tiered waterfall dropping into a pool that's open for
a swim, was the natural pairing for the same day, and the two sit close enough together to knock out
back to back without much backtracking.

**Meenmutty Waterfalls** — one of Kerala's tallest — took up a separate stretch of the day, with a
walk through forest to reach the base that turns the whole thing into a proper short trek rather than a
drive-up viewpoint. **900 Kandi Eco Park** was a quieter, less crowded add-on, a good stop for the
gorge and river views without the crowds that build up at the more famous falls. And **Kappapuzha
Dam** closed out the loop as the kind of low-key, unhurried stop that's easy to skip on a rushed
itinerary but worked well as a slower final stop before heading back.

## Getting there and around, almost entirely on points

The logistics here are worth spelling out because they made the trip as cheap as it was. Both legs of
travel — Bangalore to Kalpetta and back — were done on KSRTC, Kerala's state-run buses, redeemed
entirely through Scapia coins, so both bus journeys ended up free. The two-night stay at Zostel Plus
Wayanad and the separate Saturday-Sunday stay were also booked free using Scapia coins, which meant
accommodation for all four nights cost nothing out of pocket. The only real spend was local travel: a
rental scooter from Royal Brothers covered getting around Kalpetta and out to the various waterfalls
and viewpoints, which is the easiest way to get around Wayanad's spread-out sights on your own schedule
without depending on buses or cabs between stops.

## Best time to visit

October through May is the window that works best for a trip like this. The southwest monsoon that
hits Kerala hard runs roughly June through September, and Wayanad's trek routes — Chembra Peak
included — are typically shut during the heaviest of it, both for safety and for trail recovery.
October and November right after the monsoon are the sweet spot: waterfalls like Soochipara and
Meenmutty are running at full force, the estates are at their greenest, and the heat hasn't built back
up yet. December through February is drier and cooler still, and the better choice for anyone
prioritizing the Chembra trek specifically, since ticket demand peaks in this stretch too. By April and
May the heat picks up noticeably, which is worth factoring in if the trip includes a lot of midday
outdoor walking.
