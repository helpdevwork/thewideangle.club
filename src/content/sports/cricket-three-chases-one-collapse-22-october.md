On 22 October 2026, three international matches finished in three different formats and produced three kinds of drama: a record Indian chase in New Zealand, the second-highest successful chase in ODI history, and a Test that South Africa won by an innings after rain took two days. Here is what happened, with the numbers, and what I think each result means.

## 1. India chase 226 in Christchurch

In the first T20I of the series at Hagley Oval, India won the toss and chose to bowl. New Zealand made 225/4, with Glenn Phillips unbeaten on 82 off 40 balls and Finn Allen scoring 45 off 24 (Outlook India).

India answered with 230/6 in 19.4 overs and won by four wickets. Sanju Samson made 67 off 33 balls with eight fours and four sixes; captain Shreyas Iyer scored 42 off 28; Naman Dhir finished unbeaten on 42 off 15. India reached 50 in 3.2 overs, then lost quick wickets in the middle overs before Dhir's finish. Outlook India calls it India's highest successful T20I chase at the venue, and ESPNcricinfo's headline calls it a record chase. India lead the series 1-0.

Check the arithmetic: a target of 226 reached with a score of 230 and six wickets down means four wickets in hand, matching the reported margin.

**My read:** The conditions were good for batting, so credit the intent more than the surface. Chasing 226 means taking risks from ball one; 50 in 3.2 overs gave India the room to absorb a middle-order wobble. The real question is the bowling. Outlook's report does not give full bowling figures, but New Zealand scoring 225 after being put in suggests that India's attack will be the issue as the series moves on. The next T20I is on 24 October at the same ground.

## 2. Sri Lanka chase 430 against England

In a tri-series ODI in Rawalpindi, England made 429/4 in 50 overs. Harry Brook scored 202 off 116 balls, which Sky Sports calls the fastest double century in ODI history and the first by an Englishman; Newswire describes it as the highest individual score by an England batter in men's ODIs. Joe Root scored 69, Tom Banton 50, and Will Jacks was unbeaten on 66.

Sri Lanka replied with 430/8 in 49.5 overs and won by two wickets with one ball to spare. Pavan Rathnayake made 130, Avishka Fernando 97, Dasun Shanaka 97 off 50 balls with eight sixes, and Janith Liyanage was unbeaten on 61 off 27. Sri Lanka needed eight from the last over; Liyanage hit a six off the penultimate ball. Sky Sports and Newswire both report it as the second-highest successful chase in ODI history.

Context from Sky Sports: this was Sri Lanka's first win in the tri-series, after losing to Pakistan by 37 runs on 18 October, and England's second straight defeat after losing to Pakistan by 87 runs on 20 October. The final is on 31 October in Lahore.

**My read:** A double century lost is a bowling story. Brook said afterwards that the innings meant little in a losing cause and that England did not execute with the ball well enough. A target of 430 is only chaseable on a pitch and in conditions where par scores have moved; England's problem is that they now have to think about defending 430-plus, not only scoring it.

## 3. South Africa level the series in Gqeberha

In the second Test at St George's Park, South Africa beat Australia by an innings and 31 runs on the fifth day, 22 October local time. ESPNcricinfo's headline says South Africa came back to win despite losing two days to rain.

The scores, from ABC News: South Africa 425/9 declared; Australia 99 in the first innings and 295 in the second after following on. That checks out: 425 minus 99 minus 295 is 31. Temba Bavuma made 179 and was player of the match. Marco Jansen took 5/33 on day four, and Wiaan Mulder took the key wickets of Travis Head (121 off 190, his 13th Test century), Scott Boland and Pat Cummins on day five. The series is 1-1 after Australia's win in the first Test in Durban. The decider is the third Test at Newlands, Cape Town, from 27 October.

**My read:** Australia's 99 in the first innings is the number that decided this. Head's hundred only delayed the result; the match was lost on the first innings. Steve Smith's neck injury, which ABC reports hampered him, is a worry heading to Cape Town, and Anrich Nortje's groin injury is South Africa's.

## What ties them together

All three results came down to chasing or setting a total that looked safe. The lesson from 22 October is that in 2026 a score is only safe if the bowling can defend it. India's attack, England's attack and Australia's batting order each now have a question to answer within a week.
