There are days at a multi-sport Games where the medal count just ticks along, and then there are
days like this one, where the storylines stack up faster than the results table can keep pace with.
India went into today with medal hopes across five disciplines — weightlifting, shooting, cricket,
kabaddi, hockey, and badminton — and by the time the last mat, court, and pitch had cleared, almost
every one of them had delivered something worth talking about. Here's the full picture, event by
event.

## Mirabai Chanu completes the set

![A loaded barbell with weight plates resting on a gym floor](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1521805103424-d8f8430e8933?w=1400&q=80&auto=format)
*The snatch and clean-and-jerk cycle that has defined Chanu's career — and the one she's now medalled with at every major multi-sport stage.*

Start with the one that matters most for the history books. Saikhom Mirabai Chanu won silver in the
women's 49kg weightlifting event, and in doing so, she closed out something no other Indian
weightlifter has managed: a medal at the Olympics, the Commonwealth Games, and the Asian Games,
across every one of the sport's major multi-sport stages.

It's easy to skim past a "silver" in a results ticker, but the context is what makes this one land.
Chanu has spent the better part of a decade absorbing the physical toll that competitive weightlifting
puts on a body in the 49kg category — repeated snatch and clean-and-jerk cycles that don't get any
kinder with age, plus the injury layoffs that came with them. Completing the full house at this stage
of her career, against a deeper Asian field than most other continents can field in the lighter
categories, is as much a statement about longevity and technique as it is about raw strength on the
day. For a weightlifting program that has often lived and died by one or two individual stars, having
that full medal collection sealed is a marker the next generation coming through the junior ranks will
be pointed to for years.

## Manu Bhaker, back in the range

While the weightlifting hall wrapped up its business, Manu Bhaker was on the line in the women's 10m
air pistol event, chasing a follow-up to the double-medal run that made her the story of the Paris
2024 Olympics. That Paris result — two individual bronzes, a first for an Indian shooter at a single
Games — changed the weight of expectation around her, and every final she walks into now gets read
against that benchmark whether that's fair or not.

The 10m air pistol final is a brutal event to watch live: qualification scores get compressed into a
shootout format where a single dropped shot in the closing series can swing the whole standings. That
volatility is exactly why Bhaker's composure under it has become her calling card — the mechanics of
her stance and grip are text-book, but the temperament is the differentiator at this level. Keep an
eye on the official results feed for her final placing; regardless of where it lands, this is a shooter
building a multi-Games résumé, not chasing a one-off peak.

## Cricket gold: India 216/3, Sri Lanka bowled out for 69

![Cricket batter mid-shot, playing an attacking stroke under floodlights](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1531415074968-036ba1b575da?w=1400&q=80&auto=format)
*Smriti Mandhana's 79 off 45 balls set the tone — India never let Sri Lanka back into the final.*

If there's one result from today that needed no context or caveats, it's this one. India's women's
cricket team beat Sri Lanka by 147 runs in the final to retain the gold medal they first won at
Hangzhou 2023 — and it wasn't close at any point.

Batting first, India put up 216/3, with Smriti Mandhana the difference-maker at the top of the order:
79 off just 45 balls, the kind of innings that takes the game away from the opposition before the
middle overs even start. That platform let the rest of the order play with freedom rather than
urgency, and 216 on a final-day pitch was always going to be a stiff ask for a Sri Lankan side that
had to chase it under lights and under pressure.

They never got close. Sri Lanka were bowled out for 69, a collapse that speaks to how well India's
bowling attack executed with a big total to defend — no need to force wickets, just discipline and
consistent lines that let the required-rate pressure do the rest of the work. A 147-run margin in a
Games final is emphatic by any standard.

This is also India's first gold medal of these Games, which matters beyond the scoreboard. Opening
the gold tally with a dominant, no-ambiguity final in a marquee team sport tends to set the tone for
the rest of the contingent — and back-to-back titles (Hangzhou 2023, now this one) start to look less
like a peak and more like a program that's built real depth in women's cricket, not just a golden
generation.

## Kabaddi: back-to-back wins, 37-23 over Iran

The women's kabaddi team kept its perfect run going, beating Iran 37-23 after already having dealt
with Bangladesh earlier in the competition. Iran has traditionally been one of the tougher outs in
Asian kabaddi circles — their raiding structure and mat discipline have caused India problems in past
continental meets — so a 14-point winning margin here is a meaningful marker of where this squad's
form currently sits, not just a routine group-stage result. Two matches in, unbeaten, and playing with
enough control to put a rangy 37 points on the board against a genuine contender: this is a team
peaking at the right time.

## Hockey and badminton: still in motion

Two more fixtures round out the day, both still very much live storylines rather than settled results.

The women's hockey team takes on Thailand, a fixture India will be expected to control on paper, but
one that still needs converting — hockey has punished plenty of "should-win" assumptions at this level
before. And on the badminton court, the men's team carries momentum into a semifinal against China
after getting past Japan in the quarterfinals. China at this stage of a team event is about as tough a
draw as exists in the sport right now, and getting through the Japan tie will have told the coaching
staff plenty about squad depth heading into it.

Both results will shape how the rest of the week's medal picture looks for India, and both are worth
tracking as they wrap up.

## Where this leaves India

Step back and the shape of the day is clear: a career-defining medal from one of India's most durable
athletes, a shooter with genuine multi-Games pedigree still chasing more, an emphatic and
tone-setting gold in the team sport with arguably the highest profile back home, and two more squads —
kabaddi and badminton — showing the kind of form and depth that turns a good Games campaign into a
great one. Hockey and the badminton semifinal are the two threads still open by the time this goes to
print; check back for updates as those results land.
